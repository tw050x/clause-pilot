# Test pack

Files for testing Clause Pilot as a user would: real uploads, real AI and nothing built into the app. Everything here is generated by `npm run test-pack` (from `scripts/test-pack/`), so it can be regenerated after changes.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `library.json` | PortSwigger's approved clauses (38): mutual NDA, boilerplate and order-form clauses, in the app's export format |
| `playbook.json` | PortSwigger's standard positions (11): governing law, confidentiality term, residuals, non-solicitation, liability cap and others |
| `test-1/Brightwater-Mutual-NDA-v1-client-redline.docx` | Our mutual NDA, redlined by the client and sent back |
| `test-1/Brightwater-Mutual-NDA-v2-client-response.docx` | The client's next draft, replying to what we sent back |
| `test-2/Kestrel-Confidentiality-Agreement-client-paper.docx` | A confidentiality agreement on the client's own paper |

## Before you start

1. **Settings → AI:** set up Claude (API key) or a vLLM server. The intent check and the playbook check need it; everything else works without it.
2. **Library → Import…** and choose `library.json`. **Playbook → Import…** and choose `playbook.json`. Importing merges by id: importing again changes nothing, and a changed entry is updated (a playbook position gets a new version). Nothing is removed. **Export…** on either page saves the current set in the same format.

## Test 1: our paper, two rounds

The client is **Brightwater Robotics**. They took our mutual NDA, redlined it in Word and sent it back.

### Round 1: their redline

Create the account **Brightwater Robotics** and a contract (for example "Mutual NDA"), then open `v1-client-redline.docx` from the filesystem.

Most of the document is our approved wording, word for word, so it starts green (nothing to check). Expect to find the following.

| Where | What they did | How it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1.3 | Reworded our exclusions; same meaning | Tracked change, comment. The intent check should pass (dashed green) |
| 2.1 | Added "Affiliates" | Tracked change with a comment |
| 2.2 | Reworded our standard of care **without Track Changes**; same meaning | Marked *not our standard*. The intent check should pass |
| 2.4 | Inserted a residuals clause | Tracked insertion with a comment |
| 3.3 | Deleted our "No warranty" clause | Tracked deletion |
| 4.1 | Changed the survival period from three to five years | Tracked change with a comment |
| 5.4 | Removed "such consent not to be unreasonably withheld" **without Track Changes** | Marked *not our standard* |
| 5.8, 5.9 | Changed the governing law and the courts to New York | Tracked changes, comment on 5.8 |
| 5.10 | Inserted a non-solicitation clause | Tracked insertion |
| Parties and signature paragraphs | Their details | Not in our template (accept them) |

Use **Intent check with AI** to see which changes keep our meaning. Then review the document as you like: select text to accept or reject changes, type in the document, add comments, and use History to undo. Finish with **Marked-up copy** (save the .docx) and **Draft response**.

Round 2's file assumes we sent back the following. Any other decision is fine too: whatever differs from what you actually sent will show in round 2 as an untracked change, which is what the app is for.

- Accept 1.3, 2.1, 2.2 and the parties and signature paragraphs.
- Reject 2.4 (comment: no residuals clause), 3.3, 5.4, 5.8 and 5.9 (comment: English law), and 5.10.
- Reject 4.1, then type **four (4)** in place of **three (3)** (comment: we can meet you at four years).

### Round 2: their next draft

Review it, then create the response: **Marked-up copy** and **Draft response**. The email asks them to track every change next time, because of 5.4.

## Test 2: their paper, one round

The client is **Kestrel Health Systems**, who sent their own confidentiality agreement. It has a different structure from ours (Articles, "Section 1.01" numbering, defined terms such as "Representatives" and "Transaction") and no tracked changes. Create the account and a contract, then open `test-2/Kestrel-Confidentiality-Agreement-client-paper.docx`.

None of it is our wording, so every paragraph needs reading (grey bar, "To read"). The app helps in two ways, both on request:

- **Intent check with AI.** Each paragraph is compared with the closest of our clauses (up to three per paragraph, found by keywords when the file is opened). Paragraphs the AI finds equivalent to one of ours get a dashed green bar. Expect these to be equivalent: confidential information (1.01), obligations (2.01), exclusions (2.02), standard of care (2.03), compelled disclosure (2.04), return (3.02), ownership (4.01), no warranty (4.02), remedies (4.03), no obligation to transact (4.05), entire agreement (4.08) and counterparts (4.09).
- **Check against playbook with AI.** The whole contract is checked against each playbook position that applies to an NDA, with links to the sections each result is about. Expect deviations on residuals (2.05), the confidentiality term of five years (3.01), non-solicitation (4.04) and Delaware law and courts (4.06). The one-sided assignment (4.07) is worth spotting too: Kestrel may assign to anyone in a merger, but PortSwigger may not.

The AI only advises. Read each paragraph and **Mark as checked** (select the text to see the intent check and the button), or edit it and comment where it needs to change. Then create the response with **Marked-up copy** and **Draft response**. There is no second round.
